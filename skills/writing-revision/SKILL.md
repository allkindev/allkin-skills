# Writing revision

A first draft is never the text. Good writing is rewriting, and rewriting
works only when each pass looks for one kind of fault: the eye that checks the
argument does not see the comma, and the eye that checks the comma does not see
that the third paragraph should come first.

Revise in the four passes below, **in this order**, each one over the whole
text. Do not merge them. When the text is long, revise it section by section,
but finish a pass before starting the next.

## Pass 1 — Structure: does the text do its job?

Read the draft as its reader would, knowing only what they know.

- **The point**: can you say in one sentence what the reader must take away?
  If not, the text is not ready to be polished; fix the thinking first.
- **The opening** gives the reader a reason to go on: the point, a fact, a
  question, a scene. Never a definition from the dictionary, a "In today's
  world", a summary of what is coming.
- **The order** follows the reader's questions, not the writer's research.
  Each paragraph answers the question the previous one raised.
- **One paragraph, one idea**, with its first sentence saying which.
- **Cut** every paragraph the text holds up without. Then every sentence. A
  text loses a fifth of its length in revision without losing anything.
- **The ending** stops when the content stops: a consequence, a next step, an
  image. Never a paragraph that repeats the text ("In short", "In conclusion",
  "Ultimately").
- **Facts**: every figure, name, date and quotation is one you can stand by.
  If you cannot, mark it `[to check]` and tell the user.

## Pass 2 — Sentence: is each sentence the best it can be?

- **A verb, a subject that acts.** "The committee decided" beats "A decision
  was made by the committee" and "The making of the decision".
- **Concrete before abstract**: a number, a name, an example, a thing one can
  see. "Sales fell 12 % in March" beats "Performance was impacted".
- **Short by default**, long when the rhythm calls for it: vary the length;
  three sentences of the same shape in a row put the reader to sleep.
- **One word for one thing.** Do not swap a word for a synonym to avoid
  repeating it when it names the same thing: the reader thinks it is another.
- **Cut** the empty words: very, really, truly, quite, actually, basically,
  in order to, the fact that, it should be noted that, there is … that.
- **Precise verbs** instead of a weak verb and a noun: "decide" for "make a
  decision", "analyse" for "carry out an analysis".
- **Hedge once**, where the doubt is, not on every sentence.
- **Read it aloud** (in your head): a sentence one stumbles on is rewritten.

## Pass 3 — Machine tics: does it sound written by a person?

Texts produced by models share habits a reader now recognises at once. Hunt
them down; rewrite each one, do not just delete the word.

| Tic | Instead |
|---|---|
| Openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "In an era where", "Imagine…" | Start with the point or a fact. |
| "It is important to note that", "It is worth mentioning", "Notably" | Say the thing. |
| Everything in threes: "fast, reliable and scalable", three examples, three bullets | As many items as there really are. |
| "Not only … but also", "It's not just X, it's Y", "X isn't about Y, it's about Z" | State Y plainly. |
| Inflated words: delve, tapestry, landscape, realm, robust, seamless, leverage, unlock, elevate, empower, journey, pivotal, crucial, game-changer, testament | The plain word: look at, field, strong, smooth, use, improve, key. |
| French equivalents: « Dans un monde où », « Il est important de noter », « En somme », « En définitive », « Plongeons dans », « véritable », « incontournable », « au cœur de », « n'hésitez pas à » | Le mot simple, ou rien. |
| Dashes everywhere — like this — for asides | A comma, brackets, or two sentences. At most one pair per paragraph. |
| Colon reveals: "The result? A disaster." "The key: patience." | A full sentence. |
| A bold label then a colon at the head of every paragraph | Prose; bold for scanning only, sparingly. |
| Bullet lists for a line of reasoning | Prose; lists are for parallel items. |
| Closing summaries, "Ultimately", "In the end", a moral | Stop. |
| Rhetorical questions the text answers at once | The answer. |
| Emoji, exclamation marks, "Exciting!" in a serious text | Nothing. |
| Symmetric hedges: "While X, Y", "On the one hand … on the other" when nothing is weighed | Say which side the text takes. |
| Flattery of the reader or the subject: "fascinating", "remarkable" | Show why, or drop it. |

A text may keep one of these on purpose. It may not keep them by habit.

## Pass 4 — Surface: is it clean?

- Spelling, agreement, conjugation, the names spelt as their owners spell
  them.
- Punctuation and typography of the text's language: load the **Typography**
  competence when you have it, and apply it whole.
- Consistency: the same spelling of a name, the same format of dates and
  numbers, the same capitalisation of headings, from the first line to the
  last.
- Links, references and cross-references point where they say.

## Revising someone else's text

- **Keep their voice.** Correct what is wrong, tighten what is loose; do not
  rewrite a text into your own style. Their words stay unless a word is wrong
  or weak.
- **Keep the facts, the numbers and the commitments.** Never add a fact they
  did not give; mark what you doubt.
- Say how deep the user wants it: a proofreading (pass 4 only), an edit
  (passes 2 to 4), or a rewrite (all four). When they did not say, do an edit
  and say so.

## Handing over

1. The revised text, whole, ready to use, in the format asked for.
2. Then, short: what you changed and why, in a few lines grouped by pass —
   never a line per comma. For a proofreading of someone else's text, list the
   corrections that change meaning, and only those.
3. What you marked `[to check]`, and any choice you made that the user may
   want otherwise (the register, a cut paragraph).

## Do not

- Do not announce the revision ("Here is a polished version that…"); give the
  text.
- Do not lengthen a text in revision unless the user asked for more.
- Do not replace one tic with another (cutting "crucial" to write "key" in
  every sentence).
- Do not leave `[to check]` marks silently: each one is named in the report.
